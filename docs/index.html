THE IDEA

WHAT IS PHANTOM?

A pocket-sized learning tool for people curious about WiFi and Bluetooth security — not a black-box hacker toy. You wire the parts, flash the firmware, and see how common attack demos actually work under the hood.

WHO IS IT FOR?

Students, makers, and security beginners who want hands-on practice — not slide decks.

WHAT DO YOU NEED?

A used ESP32 board (~$4), a rotary knob (~$1), a small TFT screen (~$5), plus jumper wires and a breadboard you may already own. No soldering for the starter build.

WHAT IS IT NOT?

Not a plug-and-play weapon. Not for public WiFi, strangers' phones, or anything without clear permission.

PARTS ONLY

CHEAPER THAN BUYING ONE.

Ready-made wireless lab gadgets often cost $100–$300+. Phantom is three cheap modules and free firmware — about $10 if you shop used boards and budget screens.

  • Used ESP32 board$4
  • Rotary encoder knob$1
  • 2.8" TFT screen$5
  • Core build total~$10

WHY DIY?

You see every wire. You flash the code yourself. When something breaks, you fix it — not ship it back.

Prices are ballpark (AliExpress, local used market, spare parts bin). Breadboard, USB cable, and jumper wires not included — but you probably have those already.

HOW DO I START?

  1. Grab the code — free on GitHub, open source.
  2. Wire three parts — board, screen, knob. Photos and pin tables on About.
  3. Flash & play — turn the knob to browse, click to open a demo.
  4. Stay legal — test only on your own phone, laptop, and home network.
